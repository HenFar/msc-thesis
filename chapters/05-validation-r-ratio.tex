\chapter{Global Validation: The Massless Hadronic R-Ratio}\label{ch:validation}

As mentioned in Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}, the antenna functions that were computed
using \textit{AntCalc} in the previous Chapter~\ref{ch:workedexample} now need to be globally
validated. This validation is undertaken by the computation of the massless hadronic $R$-ratio
up to NNLO. This observable is particularly useful for validating these antenna functions
due to the fact that it is fully inclusive: it requires the entire antenna suite, whose poles
cancel only in the inclusive sum, as discussed in Chapter~\ref{ch:physicsbackground}. Its
result at NLO and NNLO is also known and has been computed and validated through methods
other than Antenna Subtraction \cite{Dine:1979qh}.

\section{R-Ratio Definition}

The hadronic R-ratio is usually defined as the ratio of the total cross-section for $e^+e^-$
annihilation into hadrons to the point-like cross-section for $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$. It is
mathematically described as follows,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        R_\text{hadr} &= \frac{\sigma(e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons})}{\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)}\\
          &= \frac{3q^2}{4\pi\alpha_\text{em}^2}\,\sigma(e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons}).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Looking closely, the $\sigma(e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons})$ term is what QCD predicts for
$\gamma^*\to\text{hadrons}$, computed via the optical theorem \cite{GehrmannDeRidder0311276,Adler1974}.
What makes the R-ratio a clean QCD observable, and a good benchmark for \textit{AntCalc}
is that the ratio features terms for higher perturbative orders as a perturbative series in $\alpha_s$ \cite{Dine:1979qh},
\begin{equation}
    R_\text{hadr} = \left(c_\text{LO}+\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}c_\text{NLO}+\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\right)^2c_\text{NNLO}+\mathcal O\left(\alpha_s^3\right)\right)
\end{equation}
here the perturbative coefficients, $c_\text{LO}$, $c_\text{NLO}$ and $c_\text{NNLO}$, respectively receive
contributions from the multiparticle final states at the relative orders via their respective
antennae, as described in Chapter~\ref{ch:physicsbackground}. For example,
$A_3^0$ as described in Chapter~\ref{ch:workedexample} contributes to $c_\text{NLO}$ \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv}.
Moreover, the leading-order term $R^{(0)}$ simply counts the number of quark colours and flavours
weighted by their electric charges \cite{Ellis:1996mzs},
\begin{equation}
    R^{(0)} = N_c\sum_q e_q^2
\end{equation}
since $R_\text{hadr}=R^{(0)}\big(1+\mathcal O(\alpha_s)\big)=R^{(0)}R(\alpha_s)$.

\section{Global Validation with \textit{AntCalc}}

As discussed, the hadronic R-ratio is a very useful quantity to compute using the
integrated antenna functions obtained from \textit{AntCalc}. As such, the programme
provides an R-ratio calculator. It is the longest-running function
in the whole package. Its result gives,

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=gray!10, linewidth=0pt]
\texttt{In[2]:= BuildRRatio[SMQCD]}\\
\hfill
\texttt{Out[2]:=}
    \begin{equation*}
        1+\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)\frac32C_F+\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)^2C_F\!\left[N\left(\frac{243}{16}-11\zeta_3\right)+\frac3{16N}+N_f\left(-\frac{11}4+2\zeta_3\right)\right]
    \end{equation*}
\end{mdframed}

As expected, the results for each perturbative order are finite. This validates the mutual 
normalisation and assembly of the integrated antennae: at each perturbative order, their 
infrared poles cancel in the inclusive sum, leaving a finite coefficient. Moreover, 
the result matches the literature's \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2004ttg,Dine:1979qh}.

\subsection{$T$-objects}\label{subsec:Tobjects}

The antenna functions computed in Chapter~\ref{ch:workedexample} are colour-stripped 
purely kinematic objects. For subtraction, as discussed in Subsection~\ref{subsec:subTerms},
we need to assemble differential cross-section subtraction terms, like $d\sigma^S_\text{NNLO}$,
from contributions across several distinct final-state topologies. For each topology, every 
relative antenna-set component must be included, correctly colour-ordered and normalised 
using the common $2C_FT_{q\bar q}^2$ factor, where $T_{q\bar q}^2$ is the $T$-object relative 
to the LO $\gamma^*\to q\bar q$ process. We define these $T$-objects as this per-topology,
colour-restored assembly.

Each antenna-set is organised in its own $T$-object, which is notated accordingly, with 
a subscript relaying the final-state particles, e.g. $q\bar qg$, and a superscript that 
relays the perturbation order as $2$ for LO, $4$ for NLO, and $6$ for NNLO. As stated, all 
$T$-objects at NLO and NNLO are normalised by the LO $T$-object relative to the $A_2^0$ antenna.
This $T$-object equals,
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar q}^2 = N\left|\mathcal M_2^0\right|^2 = 4N(1-\epsilon)q^2.
\end{equation}

The remaining relevant $T$-objects, for the coming $R$-ratio assembly, follow directly from
the integrated antennae computed in Chapter~\ref{ch:workedexample}. Writing
\begin{equation}
    P = 2C_FT_{q\bar q}^2,
\end{equation}
the NLO $T$-objects are,
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar q}^4 = P\,A_2^1,\qquad
    T_{q\bar qg}^4 = P\,A_3^0,
\end{equation}
and the NNLO $T$-objects are,
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar q}^6 = P\left(NA_2^2+\frac1N\tilde A_2^2+N_f\hat A_2^2+\left(N-\frac1N\right)\breve A_2^2\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar qg}^6 = P\left[N\!\left(A_3^1+A_2^1A_3^0\right)-\frac1N\!\left(\tilde A_3^1+A_2^1A_3^0\right)+N_f\hat A_3^1\right],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar qq^\prime\bar q^\prime}^6 = P\,(N_f-1)B_4^0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar qq\bar q}^6 = P\left(B_4^0-\frac1N C_4^0\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar qgg}^6 = P\left(NA_4^0-\frac1N\tilde A_4^0\right).
\end{equation}

We can now integrate the antennae and get the integrated $T$-objects, notated as $\mathcal T$. These 
$\mathcal T$-objects are the ones used when assembling the $R$-ratio in the next section. Let us 
now consider the two NLO $\mathcal T$-objects, $\mathcal T_{q\bar q}^4$ and $\mathcal T_{q\bar qg}^4$,
each relative to the generating processes of the $A_2^1$ and $A_3^0$ antennae. As discussed previously,
via the KLN theorem, the virtual correction poles of the first antenna must cancel against the real 
emission poles of the second. That can be seen as follows,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
        r_\text{NLO} = \frac{\mathcal T_{q\bar q}^4+\mathcal T_{q\bar qg}^4}{T_{q\bar q}^2} = 2C_F\left(\mathcal A_2^1 + \mathcal A_3^0\right)\\
        = 2C_F\left[\left(-\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}-\frac{3}{2\epsilon}-4+\frac{7\pi^2}{12}+\ldots\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}+\frac3{2\epsilon}+\frac{19}4-\frac{7\pi^2}{12}+\ldots\right)\right]=\frac32 C_F.
    \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Assembly from Integrated Antennae}\label{subsec:Tobjects}

Using \textit{AntCalc}, we are also able to compute the R-ratio step-by-step, without
having to resort to the automated function by just computing the intermediate steps
ourselves. As described in Subsection~\ref{subsec:colourAlg}, antenna functions are purely
kinematic. By taking all components, we are able to construct a process-wide
object carrying both kinematic and colour information. These are $T$-objects; their unintegrated
and integrated forms are denoted $T$ and $\mathcal T$, respectively. Conceptually,
the unintegrated $T$-object is defined as
\footnote{The $T$-objects are commonly notated by $T^2$, $T^4$ and $T^6$, depending
on their perturbative order: LO, NLO and NNLO, respectively. That is the same as
the $k$-index, introduced in Subsection~\ref{subsec:antennaFuncs} of Chapter~\ref{ch:physicsbackground}, incremented by one
and then doubled, $2(k+1)$. This
is the notation used in \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2004ttg} and the one that will be
used for the rest of the present work.}
,
\begin{equation}
    T_\text{topology}^{2(k+1)}=\text{(colour/flavour weight)}\times T_{q\bar q}^2\times\text{(colour-weighted sum of antennae at $k$-order)}.
\end{equation}
This construction takes a simple form for the $A$-type antenna relative
$T$-objects \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ATypeTObj}
    T_\text{topology}^{2(k+1)}=2C_F T_{q\bar q}^2\sum_{a} f_aA_{n, (k), a}^l
\end{equation}
where $a$ denotes the component (leading, subleading, fermion loop, etc), $f_a$
is the colour weight, that is $N$, $1/N$, $N_f$, etc, and $A_{n, (k), a}^l$ is the
relative component antenna with $n$ final-state particles and $l$ loops. For this
antenna type, this definition needs two extensions at NNLO that do not cleanly
fit into an algorithmic formula:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The $A_3^1$ antenna set represents a particularly interesting process.
    Unlike in lower order loop processes, here two very distinct kinds of
    Feynman diagram appear: those in which the loop and the emission of the
    final-state gluon are completely separate events, and those in which the
    loop involves all three external legs. The first kind of diagram maps exactly
    a $A_2^1$ antenna topology followed by a $A_3^0$ topology: first the two hard
    quarks exchange a gluon and then one of the hard quarks emits a final-state
    gluon\footnote{This is merely a conceptual, diagrammatic interpretation,
    as the actual process is much less sequential than this description suggests.}.
    Since this behaviour is not unique to the $A_3^1$ antenna, it must
    be subtracted from the bare result to retain the antenna's singular behaviour.
    When we then reconstruct the $T$-object, the $A_2^1A_3^0$ term has to be
    added back on such that the process is considered in its totality \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv}.
    \item The $A_2^2$ antenna set, also at NNLO, is the first time we find a
    double-loop topology. Here the extension required is not a subtraction of
    some kind of behaviour, but rather an addition of a new one. This set is, in
    reality, composed of two different processes: the first follows the standard
    rule closely, as it produces a leading, a subleading and a fermion loop
    component. The second, however, has a singular antenna which describes the
    self-interference of the one-loop correction with itself, now that the system
    presents with two loops.
    This antenna is denoted by $\breve A_2^2$. It is also the only
    genuinely square (not cross) term at NNLO. This means that it requires a
    normalisation of $(2C_F)^2$ \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv}.
\end{itemize}

As mentioned before, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ATypeTObj} is only valid for
$T$-objects constructed from $A$-type antenna functions. In this work we consider
all quark-antiquark antennae up to NNLO. This includes the $B_4^0$ and $C_4^0$ antennae
introduced in Subsection~\ref{subsec:antFamilies} of Chapter~\ref{ch:physicsbackground}. These
antennae populate the $T_{q\bar qq^\prime\bar q^\prime}^6$ and $T_{q\bar qq\bar q}^6$
objects, respectively.

$T_{q\bar qq^\prime\bar q^\prime}^6$ is built in the same way as other single-topology
antenna $T$-objects, like $T_{q\bar qg}^4$, with the only difference being that
here all quark flavours are summed over, requiring the inclusion of a $(N_f-1)$
term, constructing,
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar qq^\prime\bar q^\prime}^6=2C_FT_{q\bar q}^2(N_f-1)B_4^0.
\end{equation}
The identical-flavour quark-antiquark-quark-antiquark final state is described by
the sum of antennae for non-identical-flavour and for identical-flavour-only
\cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv}. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:genTSum} in the interference
term, with the identity piece vanishing by tracelessness,
we can describe the
identical-flavour $T$-object as,
\begin{equation}
    T_{q\bar q q\bar q}^6 = 2C_FT_{q\bar q}^2\left[B_4^0-\frac1N C_4^0\right]
\end{equation}
importantly without the inclusion of the quark-flavour sum term $(N_f-1)$.

With this definition of the integrated antenna $\mathcal X_n^l$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:antennaIntegral}
of Chapter~\ref{ch:physicsbackground}), we are now also able to introduce the often used
integrated $T$-object as,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integratedT}
    \mathcal T_\text{topology}^{2(k+1)} = 2C_FT_{q\bar q}^2\sum_a f_a \mathcal X_{n,(k),a}^l
\end{equation}
written schematically from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ATypeTObj}, though the structure may change
with the antenna type used to define the relevant $T$-object. This equation describes
that the integrated $T$-object, $\mathcal T_\text{topology}^{2(k+1)}$ is defined
using the integrated antennae $\mathcal X_n^l$, integrated over their respective
phase-spaces rather than integrating the composite unintegrated $T$-object directly
\cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv,Gehrmann-DeRidder:2004ttg}.

\subsection{$R$-Ratio Assembly}

The R-ratio is assembled using these $\mathcal T$-objects. We simply sum the normalised objects
order-by-order as,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        R = \frac{R_\text{hadr}}{R^{(0)}} &= R_\text{LO} + R_\text{NLO} + R_\text{NNLO}\\
                                          &= r_\text{LO} + \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}r_\text{NLO} + \left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)^2r_\text{NNLO}\\
                                          &= 1 + \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\frac{\mathcal T_{q\bar q}^4+\mathcal T_{q\bar qg}^4}{T_{q\bar q}^2} + \left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)^2\frac{\mathcal T_{q\bar q}^6+\mathcal T_{q\bar qg}^6+\mathcal T_{q\bar q q\bar q}^6+\mathcal T_{q\bar q q^\prime\bar q^\prime}^6+\mathcal T_{q\bar q gg}^6}{T_{q\bar q}^2} + \mathcal O\!\left(\alpha_s^3\right).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
Expanding the $\mathcal T$-objects, we are able to describe the R-ratio formula 
using the integrated antenna functions as,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        &r_\text{LO} = 1\\
        &r_\text{NLO} = 2C_F\,\left(\mathcal A_2^1 + \mathcal A_3^0\right)\\
        &\begin{aligned}r_\text{NNLO} = 2C_F\,\Bigg[N\!\left(\mathcal A_2^1\mathcal A_3^0+\mathcal A_3^1+\mathcal A_4^0+\mathcal A_2^2\right)-\frac1N\!\Big(\mathcal A_2^1\mathcal A_3^0+\tilde{\mathcal A}_3^1&+\tilde{\mathcal A}_4^0+\mathcal C_4^0-\tilde{\mathcal A}_2^2\Big)\\ &+N_f\!\left(\mathcal B_4^0+\hat{\mathcal A}_3^1+\hat{\mathcal A}_2^2\right)+2C_F\breve{\mathcal A}_2^2\Bigg].\end{aligned}
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Assembling these pieces together, using the integrated antenna results from 
\textit{AntCalc}, we finally get,
\begin{equation}
    R=1+\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)\frac32C_F+\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\right)^2C_F\!\left[N\left(\frac{243}{16}-11\zeta_3\right)+\frac3{16N}+N_f\left(-\frac{11}4+2\zeta_3\right)\right]
\end{equation}
which is the exact massless hadronic R-ratio coefficient through NNLO \cite{Gehrmann-DeRidder:2004ttg}
and is also exactly the expression we get by running \texttt{BuildRRatio[SMQCD]}.

\section{Summary}

The massless-antenna-function results from \textit{AntCalc} have now been globally validated in two different ways
using the hadronic massless $R$-ratio. In the first place, the fact that the three $R$-ratio perturbation 
terms, $r_\text{LO}$, $r_\text{NLO}$, and $r_\text{NNLO}$ are finite validates the correct relations in between 
the antennae at each and every order, since their poles cancel as prescribed. Secondly, the fact that the
result matches previously established results that were computed using other methods confirms that the antenna
functions are not simply correct relative to each other in a closed system, but that they are in agreement with
the wider physics. 

The fact that the \textit{AntCalc} $R$-ratio result plots close to the PDG data in the
relevant domain, and also that it fails to converge on the same data set where it was not expected to, demonstrates its
validity for what it intends to be, and that its results are physically meaningful. The fact that each order increase 
brings the plot closer to the data also demonstrates that including higher-order corrections genuinely improves 
the prediction's agreement with the physical result.
