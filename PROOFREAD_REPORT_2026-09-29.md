## C. Scientific relevance (for a supervisor at a CERN-adjacent pheno group)

**What is solid.** A working, versioned package that builds *and* integrates the complete massless final-final q q̄ antenna set through NNLO in one framework. Local checks (eikonal, AP, the triple-collinear kernels, gluon-exchange symmetry) and a global one (R-ratio to α_s², pole cancellation and the finite coefficient which I re-derived numerically). Independent Ward-identity test, and a release-acceptance protocol. You found an actual sign issue in a published expression (Ă₂², cross-checked with Jakubčík et al.). The master integrals were re-derived, not copied. The R_{8,a} derivation is a good teaching example.

**What a reviewer will press on.**
1. *Novelty vs. existing implementations.* The thesis never states what the antenna functions' integrated forms had already been available for (all of these are published), so the contribution is automation and reproducibility, not new physics. Say that explicitly in Ch1 and Ch6. Position against NNLOJET (the production antenna implementation), other subtraction schemes (STRIPPER/sector-improved residue, CoLoRFulNNLO, local analytic sector subtraction, N-jettiness, q_T) and against other automation efforts; the Introduction has none. Also state how this relates to Reis's earlier FCUL thesis (cited [28]) which the massive part builds on.
2. *Genuinely new output = massive.* Only massive A₃⁰ is done, and it is a known result (Gehrmann-De Ridder–Ritzmann). Ch6 says "some of the required massive antenna functions remain to be calculated": cite what is already known for massive NNLO final-final antennae (Abelof, Gehrmann-De Ridder et al.) so the claim is checkable.
3. *Validation of a purely-inclusive observable is necessary, not sufficient.* The R-ratio tests integrated antennae and their relative normalisation; the unintegrated antennae are tested locally only for A₃⁰ in the body; A₃¹ has no local check ("Not performed"). Numerical subtraction of a real NNLO observable (e.g. e⁺e⁻→3 jets or event shapes at NLO in a toy code) would be the decisive test, but it is outside the scope; say so.
4. *Scope.* Final–final only, q q̄ only, no D–H families, no initial-state, so not yet usable for LHC processes — the Introduction opens with the LHC but the reach is e⁺e⁻. Put that in Ch1 as a scope statement, not only in Ch6.
5. *Performance and reproducibility.* 31 min for A₂², ~1 h whole R-ratio, Mathematica-only (proprietary) with LiteRed2/FeynCalc dependencies. Fine for a thesis; the Kira/FORM/Python plan in Ch6 is sensible but uncosted. Say whether the repository is public, licensed and archived (DOI) — the footnote points to a branch, not a tag.
6. *Presentation of validation claims.* "Stringent test" and "confirms" are used often; the honest limits (massive route unvalidated globally, A₃¹ locally untested) are already stated in places — collect them in one paragraph in Ch6.

Overall: sound and honest engineering thesis with a real, checked physics result (R to O(α_s²)); the weaknesses are positioning against prior art, a few stale/contradictory statements from the last revision (items A1–A8), and a long tail of small language issues.
